# Borenstein, Bushnell and Wolak (2002) on 2019–2025 data: findings

## In plain English

In 2002 three economists showed that during California's 2000 electricity crisis, about half of what buyers paid in
the wholesale market was above what a competitive market would have charged. This repository rebuilds their
competitive benchmark, plant by plant and hour by hour, for California and Texas.

- **California now prices close to cost.** In summer 2024 the day-ahead price averaged $40.34 against a competitive
  benchmark of $43.78, and buyers paid 5.4% above competitive levels; in summer 2025 they paid 3.5% less. The
  plants most often marginal are combined-cycle stations such as High Desert (MRP Generation Holdings), Delta Energy
  Center and La Paloma, whose fuel, carbon and variable O&M cost was about $38 a megawatt-hour in August 2024.
- **The benchmark tracked California's 2022 gas shock.** On the on-peak index, production costs and the margins of
  cheaper plants account for all of the rise in on-peak spending from summer 2021 to summer 2022. Where the index and
  hourly prices overlap (2024 and 2025) the index MP runs 25 to 37 points low and drifts by 12 points a year, so it
  cannot show that market power fell.
- **Texas shows a large gap, concentrated in scarcity hours.** ERCOT buyers paid $13.0 billion above the benchmark in
  summer 2023 (75% of spending), more than half of it in about a hundred of the tightest hours. The plants most often
  marginal in the Texas benchmark are NRG's W A Parish, Midlothian Energy and Luminant's Oak Grove. ERCOT is designed
  to let prices rise above cost when reserves are short, and it fails the paper's off-season check, so the Texas gap is
  scarcity pricing and benchmark error as much as any exercise of market power.

A narrative version is in the [README](../README.md).

**Key to the labels below.** *MP* = share of buyers' payments above the competitive benchmark (paper equation [7]).
*pcomp* = competitive benchmark price, the expected marginal cost of the last fossil unit over 100 outage draws.
*W* = demand less nuclear, wind, solar and cogeneration output. *q_ff* = demand left for dispatchable fossil plants.
*musd* = millions of dollars. *hourly day-ahead* = CAISO system marginal energy component (August 2023 onward) or ERCOT
day-ahead hub average. *on-peak ICE index* = EIA's copy of the ICE SP15 day-ahead on-peak index, hours ending 7–22,
Monday–Saturday. *tight90* = hours in which q_ff reached 90% of expected available fossil capacity. *MP_offpeak_before*
= MP over the preceding December–April. Summer = June–October. Figures are `price_vs_benchmark.png` and
`lerner_by_residual_demand.png`.
